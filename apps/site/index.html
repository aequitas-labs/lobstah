Token-efficient supervision

You were promised agents that do the work. Somehow, watching them became the work: which one is stuck, which one is waiting on an answer nobody saw, which one died forty minutes ago.

Watching is why you run two agents instead of ten.

lobstah does the watching with a daemon, with no model in the loop. It tells a dead worker from a stuck one and recovers each. When an agent asks how the fleet is doing, the answer is one short read in TOON, a compact format built for agents.

So the whole fleet fits in one conversation.

Bring your own harness

lobstah is the boat, not the fisherman. Your agents do the fishing: Claude Code and Codex, signed in with your own accounts.

Mix both in one fleet and choose the harness for each job. Move a running job from one to the other with lobstah swap.

A session you already have open can sign on as a trap and take work too.

Three ways to be at the wheel

The lobstah man. One Claude Code or Codex session that you talk to. It writes the briefs, sends out the work, answers the workers, and brings you the catch.

The helm. The seat that session holds. One helm for each grounds, a set of your repos, so two sessions never steer the same fleet. A charter says what the helm may decide and what it must ask you.

The glass. A live page of the whole fleet. Each question is its own card. Pick an option or write an answer, then send.

Or no session at all: lobstah runs in the background and pings you through any command you choose.

Already run an OpenClaw fleet?

The lobstah plugin gives your fleet's agents four tools: dispatch, status, send and cancel. Operators get a /lobstah chat command that shows status without waking a model.

An agent hands the job to lobstah and stays free to talk. The daemon does the supervising.

Works with what you have

GitHub. Workers open pull requests. lobstah watches each one: checks, reviews, conflicts. It repairs a failed check or a conflict by itself, at most twice per commit. It tells you when a whole stack is ready, bottom first.

Linear and GitHub issues. Assign an issue and it becomes a job. Status comes back as comments. Reply on the issue and your reply reaches the running agent. No webhooks and no tunnel: lobstah polls.

CI and anything else. lobstah watch stands watch on anything with a command line. Its events wake you like a job would.

Your review tool. PR links open where you review: GitHub, Graphite, Linear, or your own URL.

Your pager. One config line sends pings to ntfy, Slack, or anything a shell command can reach.

The wharf: check the water from your phone

The glass, hosted. Sign in with GitHub, add it to your home screen, and answer a card from anywhere.

Your code stays on your boat. The wharf holds briefs, messages, reports and the evidence you choose to upload.

Each machine signs in as a named boat. You can revoke a boat at any time.

You are only called when a lobster is caught

lobstah brings you in for two things: a catch, or a question it cannot settle without you.

A catch. A pull request, the evidence and a report, ready to read.

A question. One card, with the choices on it. It keeps asking until you answer.

Nothing else. A dead worker is restarted. A stuck one is stopped and nudged. A trap that goes quiet while it says “working” wakes the helm, not you.

Your agents just bring home the lobstahs.

Three commands to the first catch

npm i -g lobstah
lobstah init --scan ~/src
lobstah dispatch --repo myapp --brief ./brief.md
